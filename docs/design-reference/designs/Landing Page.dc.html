AI is developed and deployed faster than governance can follow.

Scalable Governance is an applied research initiative with the goal of scaling AI governance to the pace of rapidly increasing capability and volume of AI systems, without losing the human judgment and accountability that good governance requires.

We are based at the TUM Professorship of Societal Computing.

What is AI governance?
{{ a1Sign }}

By AI governance we mean the rules, processes and controls that make sure AI systems are safe and compliant. AI governance can be understood on different levels:

  • Organisation: Companies that develop and deploy AI systems turn regulatory requirements into policies and controls that impact the AI systems.
  • Verification: Auditors, notified bodies and authorities check conformity against technical standards and legal obligations.
  • Regulation: Legislators and policy-makers write AI regulations and keep them up to date.

Most of our research so far studies the organisational level.

Why does it have to scale?
{{ a2Sign }}

AI coding agents write a growing amount of the code of AI systems. Teams build faster, and more AI systems reach the market. AI governance across all levels has to keep up with this pace. How this is going to be achieved is largely unsolved an neglecting it poses a systemic risk of AI.

Mission statement

We are building an Agentic Governance Workflow: open skills that enable effective and accountable human-AI-collaboration for governance work. We research AI system developers emperically to enable evidence-based AI regulation.

Research

We study AI governance inside the organisations that build AI systems, and among the consultants, auditors and standard-setters who interpret the rules for them. Our most recent work focuses on making governance scale: methods and agentic tools that embed regulatory requirements in AI development workflows.
