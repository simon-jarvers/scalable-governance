Agentic Governance Workflow

How to make governance scale

The problem

Most compliance budget goes into documentation for audits, not into changes to the AI system.

The EU AI Act is a management-based regulation. It mandates processes, such as risk management, documentation and monitoring, and leaves the outcomes underspecified. Governance quality is a credence goodA good whose quality the buyer cannot assess through use or inspection.: auditors and authorities cannot check by inspection whether governance makes a system safer.

Documentation is easy to inspect. Changes to the AI system are not. So the budget flows toward documentation, and compliance risks becoming symbolic: it satisfies an audit without making a system safer.

AI coding agents add pressure. Features that took weeks now take days. Governance work is still mostly manual: people read the regulation, interpret it, draft policy and produce evidence by hand. As development speeds up, governance becomes the constraint.

We want to redirect the budget toward implementation, and make the work itself cheaper.

Governance-Driven Development

Governance-Driven Development treats regulatory obligations as specifications for software development work.

In specification-driven development, a developer describes a code change in a specification that an AI coding agent then implements. Governance-Driven Development applies the same logic to regulation. An obligation is translated into a specification that a coding agent can implement and a human can verify.

Every obligation is traced to a change in the AI system, from the regulation through a policy and a control, with the evidence documented along the way.

How it works

Each obligation passes through five steps across three domains. Agents plan, implement and document. A human verifies at both handovers.

Evidence-based compliance rewards implementation over documentation. Because each change is linked to its obligation, it gives auditors a verifiable outcome to check, and makes continuous, automated checks possible.

We propose the method in our ECAF '26 paper, together with an early proof of concept at one AI company.

Agentic Governance Workflow

Open skills that let AI agents do the repeatable parts of governance work, while people make the decisions.

Governance does not bottleneck on judgment. It bottlenecks on the knowledge work around judgment: reading standards, extracting requirements, mapping cross-references and keeping documentation current as regulations change. The workflow targets that work. People keep the authority.

It separates two layers. The compliance layer holds the regulation, approved policies, applied controls and the evidence record. Only people change it. The workspace is where agents work. They use skills to decompose requirements, plan specifications, document evidence and check consistency, and every skill is started by a person.

The workflow builds on our HICSS '27 study of an agentic knowledge base for AI governance. At an AI startup, we applied it to two ISO management system standards and derived two design principles: human authority over agent moderation, and traceability.

The workflow does not automate compliance. It is an agentic harness for governance work that values changes to the AI system over paperwork. We will release the skills and the workflow as open source.
