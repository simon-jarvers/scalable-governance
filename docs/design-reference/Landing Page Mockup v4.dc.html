Research

Our emperical research focuses on the impact and operationalisation of AI governance in practice. Using participatory methods like colloborative expert workshops, we investigate governance challenges and strategies for building safe and compliant AI systems. Next, we map stakeholders across the emerging AI governance ecosystem consisting of consultants, lawyers, auditors, and standard-setting organisations. Currently, we are developing scalable approaches for agentic AI governance to match the pace of accelerating AI system development and high-risk deployment.

Our Vision

Across our studies a pattern emerges. Organisations that want to comply cannot easily tell what AI regulation requires, and the compliance artefacts they produce are often built to be evaluated by an auditor rather than to inform their own decisions. We argue that this has a structural cause: auditors and authorities have no direct way to verify that governance processes genuinely protect safety, health, and fundamental rights. This credence good property of AI governance creates a systemic pull toward symbolic compliance.

AI governance usually moves from regulator to regulated target: Standard-setting organisations (i.e., CEN-CENELEC) translate legal text into technical standards; other intermediaries (e.g., lawyers and consultants) then further interpret and facilitate the regulatory requirements for AI system providers and deployers. However, independent upstream feedback is rare. We want to fill this gap by carrying insights back to the regulator since we believe in evidence-based policy making to ensure compliant AI systems are indeed safe.

THE INITIATIVE'S GOALS
1

Assess more organisations' AI governance to better understand the impact of AI regulation in practice.

2

Build upward feedback streams to provide independent, scientific evidence to policymakers.

3

Develop methods that reduce the cost of compliance without delegating the judgment or accountability it requires.
