# AI acceleration chart: research brief

Status: data research, before any design polish. Mockup: `docs/mockups/acceleration-hero.html`
(published preview: https://claude.ai/artifact/SRh6cYk26K2vg5w8q5fSEZ).

## Why this exists

The landing page's first argument is that AI is built and deployed faster than governance can
follow. Today that is one sentence, marked "Our argument". The idea is to make it tangible with a
small interactive chart: a few buttons, each showing one dimension in which AI is accelerating,
with a headline number and its source. "Governance has to keep pace" then follows as the
initiative's own argument.

Proposed page order (not yet decided): headline → acceleration chart → "Governance has to keep
pace" (our argument) → what Scalable Governance is and where it is based → second section with the
Agentic Governance Workflow and the compliance chain.

## Evidential rules (binding)

The site's Claims Register (`docs/website-content-spec-v1.5.md` on the `feat/landing-page` branch)
governs every claim. For this chart:

- Every number is an **external claim** and must cite its source on the page.
- Prefer primary sources: the publisher's own report, dataset or paper, not news coverage or blogs.
- Self-reported company figures (e.g. "x% of our code is AI-generated") are labelled as self-reported,
  or left out.
- Each data series states what it measures, its unit, its time range, and its caveats
  (e.g. reported AI incidents partly reflect more reporting).
- No extrapolation or projection presented as data.

## What the research should deliver

1. A shortlist of dimensions (4–6) that together show acceleration across different settings,
   for example capability, compute, cost, adoption, volume of models or systems, AI-written code,
   incidents, investment. Add or replace dimensions where a stronger series exists.
2. For each dimension: the best primary source, a machine-readable series (yearly or finer) with
   the exact values, licence or reuse terms, last update date, and one sentence on caveats.
3. A data file, `data/acceleration.json` (or CSV), with one record per point:
   `dimension, date, value, unit, label, source_title, source_url, accessed, notes`.
4. A short recommendation: which 3–5 dimensions to show, and which headline number for each.

| Adoption | Organisations using AI in at least one business function: 78% (2024) → 88% (2025) | McKinsey, "The State of AI in 2025", https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai | Primary source; survey, not a census |

quoted second-hand) were left out until confirmed.
